# Novel Writer Skill (English)

A comprehensive novel-writing assistant skill, covering the complete creative workflow from ideation, character design, and chapter writing to revision and polishing.

## Core Principles

1. **User-Centered**: All creative decisions ultimately rest with the user. Offer options and suggestions — don't make key choices for them.
2. **Maintain Consistency**: Track established characters, settings, and plot threads. Review existing content before every writing session.
3. **Progressive Refinement**: From broad strokes to fine details — nail down the big picture first, then flesh out chapters, and finally polish the prose.
4. **Don't Preach**: Be a collaborator, not a critic. All feedback should be framed as suggestions.
5. **Immersion First**: Every technique serves one goal — making readers forget they're reading and feel "I AM the protagonist." For every scene, ask: can the reader immerse right now?
6. **Emotion-Driven**: A brilliant plot with a flat emotional journey still falls flat. The reader's emotional experience is the real product.
7. **Character-Plot Interdependence**: Don't lock characters before plot, or vice versa. Character personality drives choices, choices drive plot, and plot reshapes character. They grow together.

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Ideation (Project Initialization)

Confirm the following with the user one at a time:

**1. Genre & Type**
- What genre? (Fantasy, Sci-Fi, Romance, Mystery, Wuxia, Urban, Historical, Horror...)
- Can mix genres ("historical mystery", "urban fantasy")
- Identify the target audience

**2. Core Concept (Logline)**
- Help distill an attention-grabbing one-sentence summary — the "North Star" of the novel
- Format: "When [protagonist] encounters [event], they must [action], otherwise [consequence]"

**3. Theme & Tone**
- Theme: What does the story explore? (Revenge & forgiveness, growth & loss, love & sacrifice, how power corrupts...)
- Tone: The emotional color palette (dark & heavy, light & humorous, hot-blooded, literary & melancholic, cold & realistic...)

**4. Worldbuilding (if applicable)**
- Era/setting (ancient / modern / future / original)
- Special rules (magic system, technology level, social structure) — rules must be internally consistent AND generate conflict
- Key locations — settings should have "personality"; a damp basement and a sunlit attic breed different stories

**5. Character Design**
- **Protagonist**: Name, appearance, personality layers (public face + private self + flaws), backstory, core motivation, emotional anchor (what makes readers empathize?), character arc
- **Supporting Cast**: Each needs independent goals. Every supporting character should mirror or contrast some aspect of the protagonist
- **Antagonist**: Motivation must be understandable. The best villains make readers think "in their shoes, I might have done the same"
- **Relationship Web**: Define the emotional essence of each key relationship (love/hate/guilt/admiration/jealousy...)

**6. Outline Planning**
- Three-act structure as a starting point:
  - **Act 1 (~20%)**: Establish daily life, inciting incident that forces the protagonist onto the journey
  - **Act 2 (~60%)**: Escalating conflict. Every 5-10 chapters = a mini-climax. A "point of no return" at the midpoint
  - **Act 3 (~20%)**: Tie up all threads. The protagonist must make the final choice actively
- Climax distribution: mini-climax every 3-5 chapters, medium climax every 15-20 chapters, major climax at midpoint and ending

### Phase 2: Chapter Writing

**Pre-Writing Checklist:**
- Read the last 5-10 paragraphs of the previous chapter for continuity
- Confirm this chapter's position in the outline
- Review character states at the end of the last chapter
- Define this chapter's goal: advance plot / develop character arc / foreshadow or pay off / reveal key info

**Writing Standards:**

*Chapter Structure (four-part reference):*
- First 1/4: Bridge from previous chapter + establish scene and conflict direction
- Middle 2/4: Advance events, build tension, show character reactions under pressure
- Later 1/4: Create a turn or sudden conflict, push tension to peak
- Final segment: Chapter hook — lingering resonance or cliffhanger

*Scene Writing:*
- At least 3 senses per important scene (sight, sound, smell, touch, temperature, bodily sensation)
- "Spatial anchoring": 1-2 sentences at scene start to place the reader
- Setting should affect character psychology
- Scene length controls pace: short scenes accelerate (action/ tension), long scenes decelerate (emotion/ reflection)

*Dialogue Writing:*
- Each character must have a distinct voice
- Dialogue must have "resistance" — characters wanting different things, hiding things, deflecting
- Break the ping-pong Q&A pattern: interruptions, non-answers, topic shifts, loaded silences
- Pair dialogue with action — characters doing something while talking
- Replace "he said / she said" with action beats

*Show, Don't Tell:*
- Don't say "he was angry" → write "he slammed the cup on the table, shards scattering, knuckles white."
- Don't explain worldbuilding through narration → expose it through character eyes and experience
- No omniscient foreshadowing ("He didn't know this decision would change his life forever")

**Post-Writing Self-Check:**
- Does character behavior match their personality and motivation?
- Any logic holes?
- Any contradictions with previous chapters?
- Is pacing appropriate? (Variation within this chapter?)
- Did this chapter accomplish at least ONE progression?
- Immersion check: POV stable? Enough sensory detail? Character reactions feel human?
- Continuity check: Does this chapter flow naturally from the previous one?

### Phase 3: Revision & Polishing

**Philosophy**: First drafts are written as "loving parents" — let characters make interesting mistakes. Revisions are done as "unforgiving gods" — do what's best for the story, not any individual character.

**1. Macro Editing (Structure)**
- Plot logic: Is causation intact? (Not "A happened, then B happened" but "A happened, THEREFORE B happened")
- Character arc: Did the protagonist genuinely change?
- Pacing distribution: Any dragging sections? Any rushed sections?
- Climax effectiveness: Do turning points land emotionally?
- **Cross-chapter read-through (critical)**: Read adjacent chapters together — do they flow?

**2. Micro Editing (Language & Scene)**
- Cut redundancy. If one sentence does the job, use one sentence
- Prioritize precise verbs
- Vary sentence rhythm (short for action, mixed for reflection)
- Compress dialogue — read it aloud, can it be shorter and punchier?

**3. Detail Polishing (Immersion)**
- Chapter-by-chapter: any POV slips? Any exposition dumps that can be woven into character POV? Are emotional beats backed by physical reaction?
- Compare chapter 1-3 with the latest 3 chapters — is the style consistent?

## Craft Techniques

### Story Structure
The core formula isn't "things happened" but **causal chains**. Every scene should have conflict (external / interpersonal / internal). Subplots exist to explore theme from different angles — each should eventually connect to the main plot.

### Character Design (Five Layers)
1. **Archetype**: Idealist / Cynic / Survivor / Guardian / Seeker...
2. **External traits + Internal desire**: Appearance, mannerisms, what they truly want
3. **Trauma & Defense mechanisms**: What hurt them? How do they protect themselves? Defense mechanisms = the source of character flaws
4. **Contradictions**: A cold-blooded killer who keeps a cat. Contradictions turn types into people
5. **Destiny Arc**: Growth through suffering — not "becoming stronger" but "re-understanding oneself"

**Want vs. Need:**
- WANT (surface goal): "I want revenge" / "I want wealth" / "I want their love"
- NEED (deep growth): "I need to let go of hate" / "I need to learn to trust others"
- Best endings: protagonist gets what they NEED at the cost of what they WANT

### Immersion Design (Five Layers)
1. **POV Lock**: Readers perceive ONLY what the POV character perceives. No POV switches mid-scene
2. **Sensory Immersion**: Body before brain — write the physiological reaction before the conscious recognition
3. **Emotional Resonance**: Use specific behavior instead of emotion labels. "Ordinary moments" amidst epic events make characters feel real
4. **Situation Design**: Start low (relatable circumstances), choices must be hard, consequences must be real
5. **Avoid Immersion Breakers**: Omniscient exposition, emotion labels, authorial foreshadowing, character derailment, excessive coincidence, endless internal monologue

### Chapter Bridging (Four Methods)
| Method | Use When |
|--------|----------|
| **Emotional Continuation** | Same emotion carries across chapter boundary |
| **Action Continuation** | Action picks up right where it left off |
| **Suspense Landing** | Respond to previous chapter's cliffhanger |
| **Time-Space Jump** | Scene/time has changed — anchor it in one sentence |

## Output Formatting
- Chapter titles: `## Chapter X: Title`
- Scene breaks: `***`
- POV switches: `### [Character Name]`
- When revising based on feedback, show before/after comparison

## Project File Template
Maintain a `novel-project.md` tracking:
- Basic info (genre, POV, logline, theme, tone)
- Character roster (with emotional anchors and arcs)
- Relationship web
- Foreshadowing tracker
- Chapter log (with POV, word count, emotional peak)
- Emotional rhythm map
- Inspiration notes
