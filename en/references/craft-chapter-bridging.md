# Chapter Bridging

Load this when writing chapter openings or endings, handling POV switches and multi-plotline changes, or diagnosing "the chapters don't connect" or "readers have to reorient every chapter". The baseline (openings follow endings naturally; line switches get explicit POV markers and time anchors) lives in SKILL.md — as does the bridging table with the four methods (emotion / action / suspense / time-space).

> Related: POV and tense rules in [POV & Distance](craft-pov-and-distance.md); the pacing logic behind "emotional cliffs" in [Pacing](craft-pacing.md); long-form state tracking in [Context Budget](context-budget.md).

Readers should "finish one chapter after another without noticing", never reorient at every opening.

## I. Four dimensions of continuity

Before writing an opening, check each against the previous chapter's ending:

| Dimension | Check |
|-----------|-------|
| **Time** | immediately after? how much later? is it stated? |
| **Space** | same place → continue directly; new place → anchor it in a line or two |
| **POV** | same character → carry the emotion; new character → lock the new POV at once |
| **Emotion** | what afterglow did the previous chapter leave? this opening must not jump away from it |

## II. Common problems

- ✗ **Redundant bridging**: the opening re-summarizes the previous chapter at length → trust the reader's memory; at most a line or two of reminder
- ✗ **Zero bridging**: last chapter confessing in scene A, this chapter calmly eating in scene B → even one transition line keeps readers with you
- ✗ **Emotional cliff**: a heavy farewell followed by cheerful daily life → the turn can work, but account for elapsed time and the psychological process
- ✗ **Fuzzy timeline**: three paragraphs in, the reader is still guessing "when is this?" → give the time anchor early

## III. Handling time jumps

- If the skipped time is **low-value** (travel, waiting), one line suffices: "Three days later, the car stood in front of the old house."
- If **something happened** during the skipped time, it must be supplied: either a short recollection or exchange at the chapter opening, or make it a chapter of its own.
- Recollection, dreams, prophecy, and flashback need clear boundaries (a blank line plus explicit entry/exit sentences) and must be distinguished from current tense.

## IV. Switching plotlines in multi-thread narratives

- Each thread keeps its own continuity (the A-line the reader has waited for needs the clearest bridging of all).
- Switch formula: **new POV character's name or form of address + time anchor (meanwhile / three days later / earlier) + that character's distinctive concern**.
- Time relations between threads stay clear (is B "three days before" A, or simultaneous?); record absolute dates in the ledger.
- Switch in order of reader urgency, not by mechanical rotation.

## V. The ending → opening contract

Know the next chapter's first image while writing this one. Do it by **filling two slots in the chapter brief at once**:

- This chapter's ending state (emotion / location / unresolved problem)
- Next chapter's opening image (where the first sentence lands)

That way the thread holds even if you come back days later.

## VI. Boundary checklist (read the two chapters back to back when done)

- [ ] Do the last 3 lines of the previous chapter and the first 3 of this one read smoothly together?
- [ ] Are time, place, POV, and emotion all accounted for?
- [ ] Is any "bridging" done by long recap?
- [ ] If time was skipped, did anything important happen unaccounted for?
- [ ] After a POV switch, can the reader identify the new viewpoint character within three lines?
- [ ] With multiple threads, is this chapter's time relation to the others clear?
- [ ] Does this ending leave an image the next chapter can pick up?
