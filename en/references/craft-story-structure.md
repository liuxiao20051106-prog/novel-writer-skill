# Story Structure

Load this when planning an outline, building a three-act structure, distributing climaxes, or diagnosing "the plot scattered", "the climax doesn't land", "the middle sags". The baseline (causal chains; chapter ends at peak conflict) lives in SKILL.md; this is the full expansion.

> Related: [Pacing](craft-pacing.md); subplot motives in [Character Building](craft-character.md); volume-level rhythm in [Web Serial Rhythm](craft-webnovel-rhythm.md).

Lineage (paraphrased, not quoted): McKee's beat–scene–sequence–act hierarchy and value shifts, Snyder's Save the Cat! fifteen beats, and the standard three-act teaching tradition.

## I. Plot is causation, not a pile of events

Forster's classic distinction: "The king died, and then the queen died" is a chronicle. "The king died, and then the queen died of grief" is a plot.

Every advance must answer: what happened **therefore**? If you can only connect with "and then", you're not writing story.

## II. Five levels of structure (every level turns)

| Level | Scale | Requirement |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Beat** | one action/reaction exchange | smallest unit of change; use it to advance a scene |
| **Scene** | several beats | must turn, producing a small shift in some value |
| **Sequence** | 2-5 scenes | its ending hits harder than any single scene in it |
| **Act** | several sequences | its ending brings a major value reversal |
| **Story** | several acts | the climax brings **irreversible** change |

**Value shift**: every turn must be describable as "something went from + to −" (freedom → enslavement, trust → betrayal, life → death, ignorance → knowledge). If you can't say what value moved, the turn is empty.

## III. What each act is for

- **Act 1 (~20-25%)**: establish the ordinary and the lack → the **inciting incident** breaks the balance (the protagonist doesn't want to go, he has to) → the act ends with an irreversible choice.
- **Act 2 (~50%)**: escalating conflict plus change through failure. The **midpoint** is a false victory or false defeat that raises the stakes; late in the act comes the all-is-lost dark moment.
- **Act 3 (~20-25%)**: the protagonist resolves things actively using what the first two acts built — not rescued, not coincidence. The ending answers "who has the protagonist become".

## IV. The fifteen beats (three acts, refined; scale by length)

| # | Beat | Position (screenplay page → novel share) | Task |
|---|------|------------------------------------------|------|
| 1 | Opening image | 1% | show the "before" state, to be mirrored by the ending |
| 2 | Theme stated | 3-5% | someone says the thing the book will prove (the protagonist doesn't get it yet) |
| 3 | Set-up | 1-10% | introduce world, cast, and what the protagonist lacks |
| 4 | Catalyst | 10-12% | the inciting incident that breaks the status quo |
| 5 | Debate | 12-20% | the protagonist resists change |
| 6 | Break into two | 20-25% | an active decision; the new world begins |
| 7 | B story | 25-30% | usually a relationship line that carries the theme |
| 8 | Fun and games | 30-50% | deliver the promise of the premise — what readers bought the book for |
| 9 | Midpoint | 50% | false victory or false defeat; stakes rise |
| 10 | Bad guys close in | 50-70% | external pressure plus internal cracks |
| 11 | All is lost | 70-75% | the low point, often with a whiff of death |
| 12 | Dark night of the soul | 75-85% | chew on the loss in despair, then find the way out |
| 13 | Break into three | 85% | grasp the theme; devise the solution |
| 14 | Finale | 85-95% | resolve the conflict with the new understanding; settle every thread |
| 15 | Final image | 95-100% | the "after" state, mirrored against the opening |

**Usage**: write each beat as one or two sentences (who did what, what changed) — that's the spine of the outline. Discover between beats with scenes; don't pre-fill everything. Beats are rhythm, not contract — literary fiction can stretch the set-up; payoff-driven fiction can compress the debate.

## V. Mapping onto web-serial chapters and volumes

| Structure level | Serial equivalent | Notes |
|-----------------|-------------------|-------|
| Act 1 | chapters 1-3 (the golden three) | see [Opening Hooks](craft-opening-hooks.md) |
| Break into two | the big hook at the end of chapter 3 | the main-line goal is fixed |
| Sequence | one mini-arc / incident (3-8 chapters) | setup → rise → turn → payoff in each |
| Act | one volume (30-80 chapters) | volume-end climax plus a hook into the next |
| Story | the whole book | the final climax |

## VI. Subplots

- Function: explore the theme from another angle, show other sides of the protagonist, give breathing room when the main line is tight (and vice versa).
- Each one must connect to the main line: affecting the protagonist's choices, or contrasting thematically.
- No more than 3 active subplots; finish one before opening another.

## VII. Structural diagnosis

| Symptom | Likely cause | Check |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| Sagging middle | no midpoint reversal; conflict not escalating; subplots inert | which chapter is the midpoint? did the stakes rise? |
| Rushed ending | Act 3 doesn't use what Acts 1-2 built | does the final victory depend on earlier growth? |
| Scattered plot | only "and then" between events | summarize chapter by chapter; inspect the causal chain |
| Weak climax | too little suppression / unclear value shift | how many chapters of pressure precede it? what value moves? |
| Abandoned subplot | opened, never closed | does the subplot ledger record a payoff chapter? |
