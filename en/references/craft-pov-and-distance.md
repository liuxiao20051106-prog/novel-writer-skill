# POV, Tense & Narrative Distance

Load this when setting or switching POV, handling multiple plotlines, or diagnosing "the viewpoint jumped", "distance keeps sliding", "tense is a mess". The hard rules (one POV per scene, consistent POV and tense, clear markers on line switches) live in SKILL.md.

> Related: [Immersion](craft-immersion.md), [Chapter Bridging](craft-chapter-bridging.md), [Emotion & Subtext](craft-emotion-and-subtext.md).

## Fix four things once, for the whole book

| Decision | Options | Trade-off |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Person | first / third-limited / third-omniscient / second | Web serials default to third-limited; first person has a strong voice but resists multiple plotlines; omniscience costs immersion |
| POV count | single / multiple | With multiple POVs, switch at chapter or scene level only — never mid-paragraph |
| Tense | past / present | Chinese doesn't mark tense overtly, but usage of "了/过/正在" must be uniform |
| Distance | four gears below | One book can have a dominant gear plus local shifts, but never random drift |

## Four gears of narrative distance

Distance = **temporal distance** (how long after the event we narrate) × **psychic distance** (how close the reader is to the character's consciousness).

| Gear | Temporal | How it reads | Use for |
|------|----------|--------------|---------|
| **1. In-scene, intimate** | happening now | direct action, senses, half-formed thoughts | climax, peril, emotional peaks |
| **2. In-scene, near** | happening now, slight explanation | action plus one clarifying thought | most of the book |
| **3. Retrospective, middle** | after the fact | summarizes a stretch; allows evaluative tone | transitions, compressing low-value process |
| **4. Distant, omniscient** | long after / non-character view | overview, era context, cross-character comparison | book openings, historical setup, wrapping up |

**Usage**: sit in gear 2, push to gear 1 at key moments, use gear 3 for low-value process. Jumping 1 → 4 ejects the reader.

**Diagnosis**: "I was immersed, then suddenly pulled out" is almost always a gear jump, not a prose problem.

## POV rules

- One scene has only one POV character's interior. Others' minds are inferable only through externals (face, action, line).
- **No viewpoint violation**: don't write what the POV character can't see or hear, or what others did behind their back.
- **Layered knowledge**: separate what the author knows / the POV character knows / the reader already knows; no narration may overstep (also the fairness baseline for suspense, see [Suspense & Foreshadowing](craft-suspense-foreshadowing.md)).
- Legal switch points: new scene, new chapter, after an explicit separator. **The first paragraph after a switch must lock the new character immediately**: name or form of address plus that character's distinctive attention (a carpenter notices the joint; a doctor looks at the hands).
- When re-showing the same event from another POV, the second pass must add new information or a new gap in understanding; otherwise cut it.

## Extra constraints for first person

- No omniscient "he was thinking…"; other minds only through observation and guess.
- The narrator may be unreliable, but the unreliability must leave traceable clues (otherwise readers feel cheated).
- The "I" voice must be stable: vocabulary, level of self-mockery, and knowledge boundaries consistent throughout.

## Multiple plotlines

- Each line has its own **current time anchor**; on switching, the first paragraph signals "meanwhile / three days later / earlier that day".
- Switch in the order of reader urgency, not by mechanical rotation.
- Don't let line lengths stay wildly uneven for long (unless that's the point).
- Before the lines converge, each line's value shift must be clear — that's what makes the convergence react.

## Common faults

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Reader doesn't know who's watching | switch with no marker / mid-paragraph POV jump | switch at scene boundaries; lock the character in paragraph one |
| Omniscient leak | wrote what the POV character couldn't know | convert to inference, or move to a POV that knows |
| Distance sliding | summary and intimate detail mixed in one passage | separate summary from presentation, or unify the gear |
| Tense chaos | mixed "了/过/正在"; recollections with no boundary | give recollections their own paragraph with a clear boundary sentence |
| Plotline timelines don't line up | no absolute dates | keep a timeline ledger ([Continuity](project-continuity.md)) |

## Pre-write check

- [ ] Who is this chapter's POV? Are person and tense consistent book-wide?
- [ ] Which distance gear dominates? Do key scenes push to gear 1?
- [ ] Anything written that the POV character can't perceive?
- [ ] If POV switched, does paragraph one lock the new character?
- [ ] With multiple lines, where does this chapter sit on the shared timeline?
