# Dialogue Writing

Load this when writing or revising dialogue scenes, or diagnosing "the dialogue is a Q&A machine", "information dumping", "everyone sounds the same". The baseline (resistance is the core; break the ping-pong rally) lives in SKILL.md; this is the full expansion.

> Related: speech fingerprints in [Character Building](craft-character.md); intent beneath the line in [Emotion & Subtext](craft-emotion-and-subtext.md); "over-complete dialogue" in [Removing the AI Voice](craft-ai-tells.md).

## I. Six jobs of dialogue (a good exchange does at least one)

1. **Advance plot** — the dialogue *is* the action (a threat, a promise, a break)
2. **Reveal character** — we learn the person through how they speak
3. **Deliver information** — but it must flow from the character, not be read at the reader
4. **Create conflict** — resistance between characters
5. **Control pace** — dialogue can accelerate (clipped exchanges) or decelerate (a long talk)
6. **Add realism** — "realistic" is not "transcribed"; the ums and ers get cut

## II. Three layers of subtext

Every important line runs three layers at once: **literal** (the topic), **intent** (what they want the other to do), **relation** (who is on top).

Method: write the real intent first, then write a line that never mentions it (examples in [Emotion & Subtext](craft-emotion-and-subtext.md)).

## III. The core: resistance

Good dialogue is not smooth information transfer; it is transfer meeting resistance:

- A wants to ask; B won't say.
- A wants to hide; B presses.
- Both are talking about the same thing and meaning entirely different things.

Four ways to break the ping-pong rally:

1. **Refuse to answer** — silence, change of subject, counter-question, talking around it
2. **Say one thing, do another** — mouth says "I'm fine", hands give it away
3. **External interruption** — an event, a third person, the setting cuts in
4. **Answer the wrong question** — reply to something not asked (usually more dramatic)

## IV. Technique

- If the reader can tell who's speaking without "X said", the dialogue is working — that comes from fingerprints, not tags.
- **Pair dialogue with action**: characters talk while fixing something, pouring wine, packing. Far livelier than talking heads.
- Replace tags with action: "She said hesitantly" → "She set the cup down, then picked it up again."
- **Keep only necessary tags**; rely on action and fingerprint. "Smiled / sighed / shook his head" are the three great offenders.
- Read aloud; if it trips the tongue, rewrite.
- Alternate length: three exchanges of equal length read like meeting minutes.

## V. Three typical AI dialogue diseases

| Disease | Symptoms | Prescription |
|---------|----------|--------------|
| **Same-voice** | every character sounds alike, all full compound sentences | build the fingerprint table; cap each character's sentence length and give them taboo words |
| **Information dump** | "As you know, this world has seven kingdoms, and three thousand years ago…" | break information into fragments delivered through conflict; let argument carry it |
| **Over-complete** | every line polite, thorough, with a beginning and an end | add interruptions, ellipsis, half-sentences, non-answers, silence |

Also: AI loves making characters **say the emotion out loud** ("I'm so disappointed"). Convert it back to action and subtext.

## VI. Silence and white space

- Not saying it is often stronger than saying it.
- Use "didn't answer" as an answer (changing the subject *is* an answer).
- After a key exchange, leave a beat of reaction (body, setting, action) so the reader can digest.

## VII. Self-check

- [ ] Which of the six jobs does this exchange do?
- [ ] Cover the names — can you tell who's speaking?
- [ ] Any information dump to break up or convert into conflict?
- [ ] More than three consecutive ping-pong exchanges?
- [ ] Any "X said / X asked" that an action could replace?
- [ ] Are silence and interruption used?
- [ ] Read it once: does any line feel "too complete, too polite"?
