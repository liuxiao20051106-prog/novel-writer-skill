# Genre Playbooks

Load this guide after the author has selected a genre, is comparing genres, or needs to test whether the genre promise is delivered. Genre is not a fixed formula. Confirm audience, medium, length, and genre blend first.

## A shared design method

For each genre, define five things: reader promise, central question, unit of escalation, required evidence, and common failure. For a blend, name the primary promise and the supporting promise; when they compete for space, the primary promise controls structure.

## Fantasy, xianxia, progression fantasy, and LitRPG

- **Promise:** an unfamiliar world, legible growth, costly choices, and an expanding arena.
- **Design:** give powers a source, cost, limit, counter, and social consequence. Advancement should alter choices, identity, or relationships—not only numbers.
- **Evidence:** major victories trace back to established rules, preparation, or character decisions.
- **Avoid:** surprise powers, renamed tiers without qualitative change, disposable enemies, system messages replacing drama, and cost-free growth.

## Science fiction, cyberpunk, and post-apocalypse

- **Promise:** show how a change reshapes people, institutions, and possible futures.
- **Design:** define the central assumption, then derive first- and second-order consequences. Verify physical constraints for hard SF; preserve social and psychological causality for softer forms.
- **Evidence:** technology limits, resource flows, governance, and ordinary life agree.
- **Avoid:** all-purpose technology, pseudo-technical vocabulary, unlimited scarce resources, or disasters used only as spectacle.

## Romance and romantic comedy

- **Promise:** readers believe why these two people choose each other.
- **Design:** give each partner desires, fears, boundaries, and a life outside the relationship. Each advance creates understanding, risk, or commitment.
- **Evidence:** attraction, trust, rupture, repair, and commitment appear in behavior.
- **Avoid:** attraction based only on status or looks, one-sentence misunderstandings, unconditional romanticizing of control or abuse, and rivals without independent motives.

## Mystery, detective, and crime

- **Promise:** fair clues and a solution that surprises but can be verified in retrospect.
- **Design:** establish truth, motive, method, opportunity, evidence chain, and misdirection before arranging discovery.
- **Evidence:** decisive conclusions follow from information already available; forensic, legal, and police details are researched.
- **Avoid:** last-page evidence, a culprit introduced at the end, unsupported intuition, or operational instructions for dangerous acts.

## Thriller and horror

- **Promise:** escalating threat, helplessness, or destabilized perception tied to a specific human fear.
- **Design:** control what the reader and characters know, define threat rules, and remove safety in stages.
- **Evidence:** space, body, and sound details serve the threat; fear responses fit character history.
- **Avoid:** relying only on gore or jump scares, arbitrary rules, forced stupidity, and trauma as decoration.

## Wuxia, historical fiction, and alternate history

- **Promise:** personal choice collides with an era, order, loyalty, or power.
- **Design:** let martial practice express values; record historical facts and deliberate divergences. Keep institutions, travel, economics, and information flow coherent.
- **Avoid:** unmediated modern assumptions, one generic archaic voice, anachronisms, and force that ignores politics or logistics.

## Contemporary, workplace, business, and social realism

- **Promise:** recognizable pressures, relationships, and choices rendered precisely.
- **Design:** make workflows, income, power, and urban space concrete. Competence and strategy create payoff; supporting characters retain common sense.
- **Avoid:** professions as labels, invented business numbers presented as fact, unsupported claims about real people or companies, and poverty used only as scenery.

## Coming-of-age, young adult, and children’s fiction

- **Promise:** age-appropriate voice, problems, and agency; growth through experience rather than adult lectures.
- **Design:** specify reader age, reading level, and content boundaries. Young characters make choices that affect the plot.
- **Avoid:** adult voices disguised as children, imitable dangerous instructions, trauma as instant maturity, and weak safeguards for minors.

## Literary, experimental, and stream-of-consciousness work

- **Promise:** language, form, observation, or theme produces an experience that conventional telling cannot.
- **Design:** form serves theme and perception. Nonlinearity still offers orientation through pattern, motif, or emotional anchors.
- **Avoid:** equating literary value with long sentences, decorative fragmentation, and abstraction without lived detail.

## Comedy, satire, and cozy fiction

- **Comedy/satire:** define the target, character cost, and factual basis. Do not rely on punching down or repeated memes; make the work’s stance legible.
- **Cozy/healing fiction:** low external danger still needs change. Use relationships, routines, community, and small choices; do not cure trauma with one conversation.

## Acceptance check

After the outline or sample, ask whether the first chapters deliver the packaging promise, whether major turns use genre-specific conflict, whether the project has a distinct core beyond its label, and whether every blended genre has a clear role and audience.
