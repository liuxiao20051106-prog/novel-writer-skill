# AI Collaboration for Fiction

Use this guide when deciding what the AI may do, what the author must decide, and how to keep outputs original and reviewable.

## Divide the work

The author owns intent, taste, canon, risk decisions, and final approval. The AI may propose options, organize notes, draft within an approved brief, compare versions, and run consistency checks.

Before each task, state:

- the desired deliverable and length;
- the source files and their versions;
- facts and passages that must remain unchanged;
- what may be invented;
- prohibited content or operations;
- the acceptance test.

If the request is ambiguous, produce a short list of interpretations and wait for the author to choose before making high-impact changes.

## Prompt with evidence

Give the AI a chapter brief, relevant canon, the preceding scene ending, and only the context needed for the task. Ask it to label assumptions, new canon, unresolved conflicts, and facts that need research.

Prefer bounded requests such as “offer three causal fixes for this transition” over “improve everything.” Review one layer at a time: structure, continuity, scene function, prose, then proofing.

## Preserve authorship and originality

- Do not ask for close imitation of a living author or a copyrighted work.
- Describe desired traits in abstract terms: sentence density, narrative distance, humor, imagery, or pacing.
- Treat reference works as market or craft evidence, not text to remix.
- Reject recognizable copying, long quotations, lyrics, or distinctive protected expression unless permission and intended use are clear.
- Keep the author’s decisions and substantial revisions visible in the project record.

## Protect drafts and private material

Do not upload unpublished manuscripts, contracts, personal data, or confidential research unless the author has approved the service and its data terms. Minimize inputs, redact unnecessary identifiers, and keep local backups.

## Review every output

Check the result against the brief, canon, source log, and applicable platform rules. AI output is a proposal until the author accepts it. Never publish, upload, purchase, sign, or contact a third party without separate authorization.
